📏 Med Math Conversion Reference (study before you start)
1 kg = 2.2 lb
1 g = 1,000 mg
1 mg = 1,000 mcg
1 L = 1,000 mL
1 hr = 60 min
1 fluid oz = 30 mL · 16 oz (weight) = 1 lb
1 cup = 8 oz = 240 mL
1 tsp = 5 mL · 1 tbsp = 15 mL
% solution = g per 100 mL (20% = 20 g/100 mL)
Macrodrip 10, 15, or 20 gtt/mL
Microdrip 60 gtt/mL
MAP = (SBP + 2 × DBP) ÷ 3
Moving the decimal: big unit → small unit, multiply (g → mg: ×1,000). Small unit → big unit, divide (mcg → mg: ÷1,000).
Formulas: Dose = desired ÷ have × volume · mL/hr = total mL ÷ hours · gtt/min = (mL × drop factor) ÷ minutes · Concentration = drug amount ÷ bag volume · mcg/kg/min → mL/hr = (dose × kg × 60) ÷ concentration in mcg/mL · units/hr → mL/hr = units/hr ÷ units/mL.
Solving word problems: 1) Find what you’re solving for (mL? mL/hr? gtt/min? minutes?). 2) Circle only the order, weight, and concentration you need; chart data such as allergies or unrelated labs are often distractors. 3) Convert units FIRST (lb→kg, mg↔mcg, g→mg, L→mL, hr→min). 4) Round only at the end (whole numbers for gtt/min; tenths for most pump rates). 5) Ask: does this rate make sense for this drug and this client?
